# Commercial Research Brief

*Research cutoff: September 5, 2026*

## 1. Business Snapshot

Elevate Healthcare is the former **CAE Healthcare** business: Madison Industries acquired it in February 2024, and the company rebranded as Elevate Healthcare in May 2024. CAE disclosed an enterprise value of **C$311 million** for the transaction. The company is now part of Madison Medical, a privately held medical-technology group, rather than a standalone venture-backed software company. ([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1173382/000117338224000023/caeaif2024.htm?utm_source=openai))

This is a mature healthcare-simulation platform with a long product lineage, not a new entrant. The Sarasota headquarters and manufacturing site has operated since 1996, encompasses nearly 100,000 square feet, and the company says roughly 300 employees there support manufacturing, commercial, service, technology, logistics, and education. It also maintains regional offices in Canada, Germany, Hungary, the United Kingdom, and Brazil. LinkedIn displays approximately 418 employees while categorizing the company as 501–1,000 employees, so public headcount should be treated as directional rather than precise. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/about-us/our-locations/?utm_source=openai))

The most recent leadership transition is notable: **Brian Truesdale** became President and CEO in May 2024 after serving in Madison Industries’ Filtration Group. The leadership team also includes a Chief Nursing Officer, dedicated general managers for Patient Simulators, LearningSpace, and Lifecycle Management, plus a VP of Sales—an organizational design consistent with a hardware-plus-software-plus-services business. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/about-us/our-leadership-team/?utm_source=openai))

The last public financial view of the business came before divestiture: CAE reported Healthcare revenue of \*\*`192.7million∗∗infiscal2023,up27`8.0 million. Those figures are historical CAE segment reporting rather than current Elevate results, but they establish that Madison acquired an established, meaningful operating business with comparatively modest reported profitability at the time. ([cae.com](https://www.cae.com/media-centre/press-releases/cae-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-fiscal-year-2023-results?utm_source=openai))

**Maturity read:** established installed-base company undergoing a commercial and product reset under new ownership. Its current emphasis on Evo, AI-enabled LearningSpace, annual-subscription MaestroAI, lifecycle service plans, clinical education, and more disciplined territory coverage suggests an effort to increase recurring revenue, attach services, and modernize a traditionally capital-equipment-led motion. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/newsletters/the-ascent-your-go-to-for-all-things-sim/))

## 2. Product / Portfolio

Elevate sells a broad simulation ecosystem spanning physical simulators, ultrasound simulation, task trainers, center-management software, AI tools, installation and maintenance, and clinical education/consulting. It formally reorganized into four operating segments in 2025:

- **Patient Simulators**
- **LearningSpace**
- **Lifecycle Management**
- **Elevate Learning Institute** ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/newsletters/the-ascent-your-go-to-for-all-things-sim/))

The hardware portfolio is substantial. Patient simulators cover adult, emergency, prehospital, pediatric, neonatal, obstetric, clinical-skills, and advanced physiology use cases, including Evo, Apollo, Ares, Aria, HPS, Juno, Lucina, and Luna. It also sells Blue Phantom ultrasound task trainers, Medicor clinical skills trainers, and SimEquip simulated medical devices. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/products/?utm_source=openai))

**Evo appears to be the current commercial wedge.** It is a modular, high-fidelity adult simulator positioned around lower setup friction, interchangeable anatomy, wireless use, real-equipment integration, and software-driven physiological response. Public hiring language shows a specific clinical sales-specialist role for Evo, supporting demos, early-adopter outreach, and “Super 80” high-value accounts. That focus is more concrete than the company’s broader ecosystem messaging. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/product/evo/?utm_source=openai))

**LearningSpace is the portfolio’s clearest software and expansion product.** It combines audiovisual recording, simulation-center scheduling, assessments, checklists, surveys, reporting, OSCE administration, and debriefing. The current packaging distinguishes Essentials from Enterprise; Enterprise adds advanced reporting, resource tracking, OSCE management, and broader ecosystem integration. Critically, it is positioned as simulator-agnostic and explicitly supports Elevate, Laerdal, and Gaumard equipment. That makes LearningSpace viable both as an attach product to simulator sales and as a displacement/coexistence play within heterogeneous simulation centers. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/product/learningspace/?utm_source=openai))

The company is also moving AI into two distinct workflows:

- **Ehli / LearningSpace AI Assistant** supports transcript generation, checklist scoring suggestions, source-video retrieval, and assessment review.
- **MaestroAI** supports scenario drafting and AI patient conversations within compatible Elevate simulators. MaestroAI is explicitly sold as an annual subscription and requires an active SimPort account, compatible simulator, and connected Maestro software. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/product/learningspace/?utm_source=openai))

The portfolio story is strongest where products reinforce a simulation-center workflow: a simulator or standardized patient encounter generates the experience; LearningSpace records, coordinates, scores, and debriefs it; the Learning Institute helps faculty operate it; Lifecycle Management protects uptime. It is less proven publicly that customers routinely buy the entire stack in one coordinated enterprise program. Most recent case studies demonstrate individual products or local bundles rather than broad, multi-site enterprise-standardization deployments. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/newsletters/the-ascent-your-go-to-for-all-things-sim/))

Partnerships extend breadth without requiring Elevate to build every modality itself. SimX provides a VR referral path and scenario library, while Paradigm Medical Systems expands Elevate’s OB/GYN task-trainer catalog. These are sensible portfolio adjacencies, but they likely require distinct selling and enablement motions from the core patient-simulator and LearningSpace businesses. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/media-releases/elevate-healthcare-partners-with-virtual-reality-healthcare-training-specialists-simx-to-launch-next-gen-healthcare-training-solutions/?utm_source=openai))

## 3. Customers & Customer Base

### Selected customer evidence

**Imperial Valley College — LearningSpace-centered nursing and allied-health simulation program**

- Imperial Valley College uses LearningSpace alongside Apollo, Aria, Juno, Lucina, and Luna simulators in its simulation lab.
- Dr. Daniel Ortiz, Associate Dean of Nursing and Allied Health, described LearningSpace as enabling real-time viewing, recording, review, and debriefing for students who otherwise could not see demonstrations effectively.
- The vendor-authored case study reports that the college moved from two or three simulations per week to multiple cohorts per day over three years; it also reported more than 20 scenarios in one semester across settings including ICU, med-surg, obstetrics, and school nursing. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/customer-stories/imperial-valley-college-improves-healthcare-education-with-learningspace/?utm_source=openai))

**Sherry Lesar School of Nursing, Montana Technological University — multi-simulator nursing-center deployment**

- A major donor-funded expansion converted a one-room program into a 5,000-square-foot center with four simulation rooms, four debriefing rooms, a telehealth room, nursing station, and medication room.
- The center uses Lucina alongside Apollo and SimEquip. The nursing director said the combination allowed instructors to increase scenario number and complexity without adding simulation or clinical hours.
- This is a strong example of a hardware-led land expanding into a broader simulation environment, although the outcome claims are from a vendor-published customer story rather than independently audited research. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/customer-stories/sherry-lesar-school-of-nursing-at-montana-tech-revolutionizes-nursing-education-with-elevate-healthcares-simulation-solutions/?utm_source=openai))

**Anderson College of Health, Business and Technology — Vimedix ultrasound education**

- The Toronto-based college selected Vimedix because conventional scanners mainly allowed practice on normal anatomy, while the program needed pathology exposure and more realistic OB/GYN and endovaginal scanning practice before rotations.
- Its program chair said Vimedix became integral to student preparation for clinical placements. The school also identified a planned echocardiography program as a future use for Vimedix cardiac content, providing an unusually clear specialty-expansion path. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/customer-stories/anderson-college-of-health-business-and-technology-utilizes-elevate-healthcares-ultrasound-simulator-to-score-hands-on-training/?utm_source=openai))

**University of Alabama at Birmingham — AI-assisted clinical-skills assessment pilot**

- UAB’s Office of Standardized Patient Education collaborated with Elevate to evaluate AI-graded transcripts for checklist performance in clinical-skills assessments.
- Elevate’s public webinar says the validation involved 59 learners and compared AI scoring with standardized-patient, video-observer, and transcript-review methods, reporting high inter-rater agreement meeting the center’s assessment standards.
- This is meaningful early evidence for Ehli as a faculty-capacity and quality-assurance tool, but it remains a collaborative pilot rather than broad proof of scaled production deployment across many institutions. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/uncategorized/artificial-intelligence-ai-in-clinical-skills-assessments/?utm_source=openai))

**Mayo Clinic College of Medicine and Science — LearningSpace expansion**

- CAE’s fiscal-2023 report said it expanded its relationship with Mayo Clinic College of Medicine and Science through a significant LearningSpace partnership for the Rochester, Minnesota simulation center.
- The disclosure validates LearningSpace relevance in a sophisticated academic-medical setting, though public materials do not disclose deployment scope, contract value, adoption metrics, or clinical outcome evidence. ([cae.com](https://www.cae.com/media/documents/FY23_Global_Annual_Activity_and_Sustainability_Report_-_EN.pdf?utm_source=openai))

### Customer base analysis

Public evidence is most concentrated in **academic healthcare education**: nursing schools, medical schools, community colleges, clinical-skills centers, and multi-disciplinary university simulation programs. The company also explicitly serves hospitals, EMS, government/military, emergency response, allied-health programs, and medical-device manufacturers, but recent named proof is much thinner in hospital-system-wide training, government procurement, or device-manufacturer applications. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/about-us/our-customers/?utm_source=openai))

The customer archetype that recurs is not simply “healthcare institution.” It is an organization that has a formal simulation program, clinical curriculum, standardized-patient workflow, or multidisciplinary skills-lab operation—and enough learner volume, scenario complexity, faculty burden, or facility scale to justify capital equipment plus operational tooling. High Point University’s eight-plus health-program simulation operation and Imperial Valley College’s multi-cohort workflow illustrate that pattern particularly clearly. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/webinars/the-real-sim-center-high-point/?utm_source=openai))

Geographically, the named proof spans the United States, Canada, Europe, India, and Brazil, consistent with the company’s regional office footprint. The deepest public proof, however, is North American and education-led. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/about-us/our-locations/?utm_source=openai))

## 4. Market / Competition

**Laerdal Medical**

- Laerdal is a major incumbent in patient simulation and is a direct alternative in nursing, hospital, EMS, and government training environments. It also distributes SimX VR in the United States and Canada for hospitals, EMS, and government customers.
- Elevate’s LearningSpace supports Laerdal hardware, creating a coexistence path where Elevate can sell center-management software into installed Laerdal environments rather than requiring a full simulator replacement.
- In capital-equipment opportunities, Laerdal is likely a head-to-head hardware competitor; in LearningSpace opportunities, the posture can be augmentation or operational-layer displacement. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/product/learningspace/?utm_source=openai))

**Gaumard Scientific**

- Gaumard overlaps across patient simulators, task trainers, ultrasound, mixed reality, learning modules, and video-assisted debriefing.
- Its 75-plus years in simulation, broad catalog, and dedicated technical-support infrastructure make it a credible alternative in core manikin and skills-lab purchases.
- As with Laerdal, LearningSpace’s stated support for Gaumard equipment gives Elevate a route to coexist with a competitor’s installed base. ([gaumard.com](https://www.gaumard.com/?utm_source=openai))

**SimX**

- SimX is not currently best understood as a direct Elevate competitor. Elevate announced a 2025 partnership through which its customers can be referred to SimX for VR scenarios, while Elevate contributes physical simulation, center management, and educational expertise.
- The relationship widens Elevate’s modality story, but SimX’s separate platform means the integration and commercial ownership model need validation. It is not clear publicly whether Elevate earns resale revenue, referral economics, or only strategic portfolio breadth. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/media-releases/elevate-healthcare-partners-with-virtual-reality-healthcare-training-specialists-simx-to-launch-next-gen-healthcare-training-solutions/?utm_source=openai))

**Specialist virtual simulation vendors**

- Oxford Medical Simulation, SimX, and other digital-simulation providers remain relevant substitutes when a buyer prioritizes scalable scenario access, remote delivery, or lower physical-infrastructure requirements over tactile procedural realism. The 2026 Society for Simulation in Healthcare product guide lists these vendors alongside Laerdal and Gaumard, underscoring that buyers are evaluating multiple modalities rather than one monolithic “simulation” category. ([ssih.org](https://ssih.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/2026_Unbound_Product%20Guide_LR.pdf?utm_source=openai))

The most serious competitor is often the **status quo**:

- Existing manikins and AV systems that are “good enough.”
- Faculty-built scenarios, manual video review, spreadsheet scheduling, paper checklists, and decentralized assessment processes.
- Standardized-patient programs for communication and OSCE work.
- Conventional ultrasound machines used on normal volunteers or anatomy models.
- Delaying a center upgrade until grant, donor, capital-budget, accreditation, or facility-expansion funding is available.

The company’s strongest route around status quo inertia is not claiming superior simulation realism in the abstract. It is showing that the existing workflow limits learner access, faculty capacity, assessment consistency, or use of already-owned simulation assets.

## 5. ICP / Priority Accounts

The highest-priority operating archetype appears to be the **multi-program academic or academic-medical simulation center**: an institution serving nursing plus additional disciplines, operating multiple rooms or sites, using standardized patients or OSCEs, and facing recurring scheduling, recording, assessment, faculty-capacity, or equipment-utilization problems.

Attractive accounts tend to have:

- Multiple health-profession programs sharing simulation infrastructure.
- A defined simulation director, clinical-skills leader, nursing/allied-health dean, or center-management team.
- Meaningful learner throughput and a need to run repeatable scenarios across cohorts.
- Capital plans, new facility development, curriculum expansion, accreditation needs, grants, donor funding, or a mandate to increase clinical-readiness capacity.
- A visible need to connect physical simulation with objective documentation, longitudinal performance data, and debriefing.
- Existing multi-vendor simulator estates, where LearningSpace can land without forcing a wholesale equipment replacement. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/customer-stories/imperial-valley-college-improves-healthcare-education-with-learningspace/?utm_source=openai))

There are at least two distinct product-led ICPs:

- **Patient simulators / Evo:** nursing, emergency-care, EMS, and healthcare-education programs replacing aging equipment, opening a new lab, or expanding capacity. The business case hinges on realistic hands-on practice, durability, configurability, and avoiding downtime.
- **LearningSpace / Ehli:** larger or more complex simulation programs with recurring AV debriefing, OSCE, standardized-patient, scheduling, video-review, and assessment workload. The business case is operational standardization and faculty leverage more than realism alone. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/product/evo/?utm_source=openai))

Hospitals with active in-situ training, nursing onboarding, obstetric safety work, or clinical-quality initiatives are strategically attractive, but public evidence does not yet show the same density of repeatable hospital-system wins as it does in academia. That should make hospital expansion an evidence-validation priority, not an assumed mature repeatable motion. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/about-us/our-customers/?utm_source=openai))

Weak-fit accounts are likely small, single-program labs with low scenario frequency, limited operating staff, no funded facility plan, and no meaningful assessment or cross-disciplinary workflow. They may have genuine training needs but lack the scale and administrative complexity needed to support a full ecosystem sale.

## 6. Buying Group

The buying group changes by product, but the public evidence points to a recurring architecture:

- **End users:** faculty, clinical educators, simulation operations staff, lab coordinators, simulation technicians, standardized patients, and learners.
- **Operational owner and champion:** simulation director, director of clinical skills, associate dean of nursing/allied health, program chair, or hospital education leader. These are the roles most visible in customer examples.
- **Economic buyer:** dean, health-sciences executive, hospital education leader, department chair, or broader academic/hospital capital-budget owner. In larger projects, a donor, grant sponsor, facilities leader, or system executive may materially shape the purchase.
- **Clinical stakeholders:** nursing leadership, specialty faculty, EMS educators, OB/GYN, respiratory therapy, ultrasound, anesthesia, emergency medicine, and clinical-quality teams, depending on the simulator and curriculum.
- **IT, security, and governance:** IT and AV teams matter particularly for LearningSpace, cloud-connected products, networked simulators, recorded learner sessions, and AI-enabled transcript workflows. LearningSpace support materials reference networking, storage, backups, and systems connectivity; MaestroAI requires connected simulators and an active organizational account. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/newsletters/the-ascent-your-go-to-for-all-things-sim/))
- **Likely blockers:** incumbent-equipment owners, faculty concerned about workflow disruption or AI reliability, procurement, legal/security reviewers, and budget holders who see simulation as a discretionary lab upgrade rather than a capacity or quality system.

Trigger events are unusually legible: new program launches, nursing-enrollment growth, facility expansion, accreditation preparation, replacement of outdated simulators, a need to standardize OSCEs, grant or donor availability, faculty grading burden, and a patient-safety or emergency-readiness initiative. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/customer-stories/sherry-lesar-school-of-nursing-at-montana-tech-revolutionizes-nursing-education-with-elevate-healthcares-simulation-solutions/?utm_source=openai))

Buying autonomy is often fragmented. A simulation director may be the strongest champion but may not control capital expenditure, IT approval, facilities redesign, or ongoing maintenance budgets. Elevate’s sales process therefore needs to help the local champion assemble a cross-functional case, not merely win an educator’s product preference.

## 7. Commercial Motion

Elevate appears to operate a **field-led, consultative capital-equipment motion** with software, service, and education attachments.

Pipeline creation appears to combine:

- Territory sales managers and regional sales leadership.
- Hands-on product demonstrations and field events.
- Virtual demos, webinars, customer-story marketing, simulation-community events, and educational programming.
- Clinical specialists who add domain credibility to major opportunities.
- Relationships with academic, hospital, and simulation-association communities. ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/east-coast-regional-sales-executive-at-elevate-healthcare-4444151867))

The public sales-director role identifies strategic priorities as growth in **patient simulators, LearningSpace, and Lifecycle Management**, with Territory Sales Managers, Strategic Account Managers, Sales Operations, Project Management, distributors, partners, and integrators involved in the motion. It also references a “Super 80” priority-account approach, indicating deliberate account concentration rather than a purely broad-based catalog sales model. ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/east-coast-regional-sales-executive-at-elevate-healthcare-4444151867))

The likely deal sequence is:

1. A facility, curriculum, readiness, or operational trigger creates interest.
2. Elevate diagnoses the simulation environment and demonstrates the relevant simulator, workflow, or software.
3. A clinical specialist and/or educator validates use-case fit and helps translate features into curriculum, learner, or faculty outcomes.
4. The buyer builds a capital, grant, donor, or operating-budget case.
5. IT/AV/security review intensifies for LearningSpace, AI, and recorded-data workflows; facilities and project management become more important for multi-room installations.
6. Procurement follows through direct purchasing, cooperative agreements, or formal institutional approvals.
7. Installation, education, service, and ongoing support determine whether the product becomes operationally embedded. ([caehealthcare.com](https://www.caehealthcare.com/learningspace/services/?utm_source=openai))

LearningSpace and AI are likely to have a different validation path from Evo. Buyers can evaluate workflow fit, scoring quality, integration, data handling, and faculty adoption through a pilot or targeted use case—such as OSCE transcript review—before considering broad deployment. UAB’s 59-learner validation study is an example of the proof a sophisticated clinical-skills program may require. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/webinars/the-real-sim-center-birmingham/?utm_source=openai))

## 8. Economics / Pricing / Value

Elevate does not publish list pricing for core patient simulators, Vimedix, LearningSpace, major installations, consulting, or MaestroAI. However, public sources provide meaningful packaging and procurement clues.

- **MaestroAI** is sold as an annual subscription, though licensing levels and prices are not public.
- **LearningSpace** is packaged as Essentials and Enterprise, with Enterprise oriented to more complex centers and OSCE/reporting requirements.
- A Sourcewell cooperative-purchasing agreement provides participating agencies a **5% discount off list price** for simulators, related installation, training, support, and maintenance; LearningSpace extended warranty is excluded from that discount and requires a supplier agreement.
- Select task trainers and accessories are sold directly online, with public prices ranging from hundreds to tens of thousands of dollars. Evo AuRA, for example, is listed at **`2,995∗∗,andanechocardiography/pericardiocentesistrainerislistedat∗∗`****43,823.83**. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/product/maestroai/?utm_source=openai))

A public community-college board packet gives useful order-of-magnitude context for full simulator deployments. Moraine Valley Community College approved **$215,628.56** in November 2025 for two Evo simulators, one Aria pediatric simulator, associated installation, equipment, and training through the Sourcewell agreement. This is a specific transaction, not a general price card, but it confirms that meaningful patient-simulator purchases are six-figure capital decisions with training and implementation attached. ([morainevalley.edu](https://www.morainevalley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/PRESS-PACKET-November-20-2025.pdf))

The strongest value buckets vary by offering:

- **Patient simulators:** learner readiness, repeatable exposure to high-risk or rare events, greater scenario range, equipment durability, and less downtime.
- **Vimedix / ultrasound:** earlier hands-on practice, pathology exposure before clinical rotations, and more repeatable scanning experience than normal-anatomy practice alone.
- **LearningSpace / Ehli:** reduced manual video-review and scoring burden, more consistent assessments, better debriefing evidence, and operational coordination across rooms, programs, and cohorts.
- **Lifecycle Management:** simulator uptime, maintenance predictability, and protection of an already-large capital investment.
- **Learning Institute / Achieve:** adoption, faculty confidence, program design, and the conversion of technology purchase into a functioning training program. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/customer-stories/anderson-college-of-health-business-and-technology-utilizes-elevate-healthcares-ultrasound-simulator-to-score-hands-on-training/?utm_source=openai))

Value realization is not automatic. A simulator creates limited benefit if scenarios are not integrated into curriculum, faculty cannot run or debrief sessions consistently, the equipment is down, or learner volume is too low. Likewise, LearningSpace and AI value depends on well-designed checklists, governance over scoring, faculty review, reliable recording infrastructure, and use of data in program improvement. Elevate itself emphasizes faculty-in-the-loop review for its AI products, which is a more credible posture than promising full assessment automation. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/product/maestroai/?utm_source=openai))

## 9. Expansion Paths

The most coherent expansion sequence is:

1. **Land with a patient simulator or targeted specialty trainer** during a program launch, lab expansion, curriculum refresh, or capital replacement.
2. **Attach implementation, education, and Lifecycle service** to protect utilization and reduce operational failure.
3. **Add LearningSpace** once the program has enough rooms, cohorts, standardized-patient encounters, or debriefing/assessment complexity to justify a common operating layer.
4. **Add AI tools** where scenario authoring, learner communication practice, OSCE administration, or review workload becomes the constraint.
5. **Expand by specialty and modality**: OB/GYN, neonatal, ultrasound, EMS, respiratory care, emergency medicine, or clinical skills. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/newsletters/the-ascent-your-go-to-for-all-things-sim/))

Expansion by **site or program** is particularly plausible in universities and health systems that operate multiple campuses, disciplines, or simulation rooms. High Point University’s multi-program operation and the prior CAE report of a multi-location India sale show why a standardized data and operations layer could support broader deployment. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/webinars/the-real-sim-center-high-point/?utm_source=openai))

Expansion by **workflow** is strongest for LearningSpace: recording/debriefing can lead to scheduling, assessment, reporting, standardized-patient administration, inventory management, and AI-assisted review. This adjacency reuses the same operational owner and existing learner-event data. ([caehealthcare.com](https://www.caehealthcare.com/solutions/brands/cae-learningspace/?utm_source=openai))

VR through SimX is an adjacent modality but likely requires a partially distinct motion. It can extend scenario breadth and remote-access possibilities, yet may have a different buyer, budget line, infrastructure profile, and competitive set than an installed manikin or center-management system. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/media-releases/elevate-healthcare-partners-with-virtual-reality-healthcare-training-specialists-simx-to-launch-next-gen-healthcare-training-solutions/?utm_source=openai))

## 10. Commercial Organization

The commercial organization appears deliberately built around complex field selling rather than self-service product conversion.

- The VP of Sales is supported by regional sales leadership and Territory Sales Managers.
- The current regional sales-leader role calls for hands-on field coaching, CRM discipline, pricing discussions, strategic account coverage, and coordination with Sales Operations, Strategic Account Managers, distributors, integrators, project management, customer service, and product leaders.
- Dedicated clinical sales specialists support Evo demonstrations and adoption, indicating that clinical credibility is part of the sale—not merely post-sale education.
- The Elevate Learning Institute is led through the Chief Nursing Officer’s organization and provides clinical educators, program development, product training, consulting, and outcome-oriented education.
- Lifecycle Management provides service, installation, technical support, maintenance plans, and customer-experience resources. ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/east-coast-regional-sales-executive-at-elevate-healthcare-4444151867))

This resource model implies that higher-value deals are not lightweight transactions. Physical deployment, clinical use-case design, faculty adoption, AV/IT setup, maintenance, and procurement can all shape time-to-value. The business can defend revenue through service and expertise, but it must continually ensure that the human support required to land and expand accounts does not constrain growth or obscure product economics.

## 11. Commercial Read

### What looks strong

- **A credible installed-base and product heritage.** The former CAE Healthcare business brings decades of simulation experience, a global service footprint, manufacturing capability, and historical scale that newer software-only simulation companies cannot easily replicate. ([cae.com](https://www.cae.com/media-centre/press-releases/cae-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-fiscal-year-2023-results?utm_source=openai))
- **LearningSpace gives Elevate a valuable control point beyond hardware.** Its simulator-agnostic posture allows the company to sell into mixed fleets and makes the software a plausible recurring-revenue, data, and expansion layer rather than merely a bundled accessory. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/product/learningspace/?utm_source=openai))
- **The company has tangible evidence of customer workflow impact.** Imperial Valley College, Montana Tech, Anderson College, Mayo Clinic, and UAB each demonstrate a specific operational or educational use case rather than generic logo placement. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/customer-stories/sherry-lesar-school-of-nursing-at-montana-tech-revolutionizes-nursing-education-with-elevate-healthcares-simulation-solutions/?utm_source=openai))
- **The newer commercial focus is more disciplined than the broad catalog suggests.** Evo, LearningSpace, Lifecycle Management, high-value accounts, and clinical specialist support indicate an effort to focus resources around a few commercially important motions. ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/east-coast-regional-sales-executive-at-elevate-healthcare-4444151867))

### What I would question

- **The “full ecosystem” narrative is ahead of the public proof base.** The company can credibly supply the elements of an ecosystem, but recent customer evidence is concentrated in academic institutions and discrete products. Public evidence of system-wide hospital standardization, recurring multi-site software rollouts, or broad adoption of the entire Elevate stack remains limited.
- **Hospital and health-system opportunity is more asserted than demonstrated.** The company clearly has hospital-oriented offerings and consulting, but its strongest visible customer stories center on schools and university simulation programs. The key question is whether academic sales are the core growth engine or merely the public-facing evidence of a broader enterprise motion. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/about-us/our-customers/?utm_source=openai))
- **AI has credible early validation but not yet broad commercial proof.** UAB’s 59-learner study is encouraging, and faculty-in-the-loop positioning is sensible. Still, public evidence does not yet establish recurring adoption, measurable faculty-hour savings across many accounts, AI pricing power, or successful enterprise governance at scale. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/webinars/the-real-sim-center-birmingham/?utm_source=openai))
- **Capital-equipment economics impose a high proof burden.** The six-figure Moraine Valley deployment, cooperative purchasing mechanics, installation, training, and service requirements all imply multi-stakeholder purchases that may be episodic and budget-cycle dependent. ([sourcewell-mn.gov](https://www.sourcewell-mn.gov/cooperative-purchasing/011822-CAE))

### Key unknowns

- What percentage of current revenue comes from patient simulators, LearningSpace, services, consumables, education, and recurring maintenance or subscription revenue?
- How much of the customer base is academic versus hospital, EMS, government/military, and medical-device manufacturers?
- Does LearningSpace reliably land as a standalone cross-vendor product, or is it primarily attached to Elevate hardware?
- What are the actual adoption, retention, and expansion metrics for LifeSpan, Ehli, and MaestroAI?
- How repeatable is the “Super 80” account strategy, and what distinguishes those accounts beyond current internal prioritization? ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/east-coast-regional-sales-executive-at-elevate-healthcare-4444151867))

### Overall read

Elevate is really a long-established simulation-equipment company attempting to become a more integrated **clinical-readiness infrastructure provider**. The strategic asset is not just a manikin catalog; it is the ability to combine physical realism, training expertise, center operations, assessment data, service, and increasingly AI-supported faculty workflows.

The most attractive near-term commercial path is likely not a generalized “sell every modality to every healthcare organization” strategy. It is winning high-complexity simulation programs where a capital purchase can open a broader workflow, service, and software relationship. The key test is whether Elevate can turn that bundled value into repeatable, measurable account expansion rather than relying on periodic equipment refreshes.

## 12. Messaging Reframe & Bottom Line

### Current positioning

Elevate publicly frames itself as a healthcare-simulation leader delivering physical, virtual, and digital solutions that improve patient safety, clinical performance, workforce readiness, and healthcare education. Its current vision is to create the “most realistic, accessible, and autonomous Simulation Ecosystem,” with a consistent emphasis on preparing clinicians for “moments that matter.” ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/about-us/?utm_source=openai))

### Teach

The most useful insight for a buyer is that the principal constraint on clinical readiness is often **not a lack of simulation equipment**. It is the inability to consistently create scenarios, schedule people and rooms, capture what occurred, assess performance, deliver timely feedback, and demonstrate program value across growing cohorts.

Imperial Valley College’s shift from a few simulations weekly to multiple cohorts per day, High Point’s coordination across eight-plus programs, and UAB’s assessment-validation work all point to the same issue: simulation becomes strategic when it is run as an operating system for readiness, not as an isolated lab activity. ([elevatehealth.net](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/customer-stories/imperial-valley-college-improves-healthcare-education-with-learningspace/?utm_source=openai))

### Reframe

- **Current view:** “We need better simulation equipment to improve clinical training.”
- **More useful view:** “We need a repeatable readiness system that lets the same faculty, rooms, and clinical content produce more consistent practice, assessment, and improvement.”
- **Changed buying criteria:** Buyers should evaluate not only realism and device specifications, but also faculty workload, interoperability with existing assets, assessment consistency, uptime, implementation support, and the ability to scale across programs or sites.

### Bottom line

Elevate is selling more than manikins: it is selling the operating capacity to turn clinical simulation into repeatable, evidence-producing readiness work. It appears most likely to win where a multi-program academic or academic-medical simulation center has enough complexity that disconnected hardware, manual assessment, and fragmented operations have become the real problem.

A commercial operator should focus first on proving the **LearningSpace-plus-services expansion motion** around high-value simulator deployments: that is where the company can move from episodic capital equipment sales to a more durable operational relationship.

The single biggest unresolved question is whether Elevate has comparable repeatable traction in hospital-system clinical readiness and quality workflows—or whether academia remains the true commercial center of gravity.

**One sentence to remember before the meeting:** *Elevate’s opportunity is to stop being evaluated as a simulator vendor and start being evaluated as the infrastructure that allows constrained clinical-education teams to scale readiness without proportionally scaling faculty, rooms, or administrative burden.*
